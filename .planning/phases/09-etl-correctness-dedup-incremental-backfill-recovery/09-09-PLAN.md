**D-17 scope interpretation, confirmed explicitly:** D-17 is LOCKED as "stuck-lease reclaim stays
fully automatic for all 3 stages... no operator confirmation gate for any stage, including
publish." This plan deliberately does NOT give `DBT_BUILD` a literal lease-expiry/reclaim timer the
way `STAGE_LOAD`/`PUBLISH` have (via `claim_run_stage`'s `lease_expires_at` + `ON CONFLICT ...
WHERE ... lease_expires_at < now()` reclaim). Reasoning: a lease-expiry reclaim exists for
`STAGE_LOAD`/`PUBLISH` specifically because those are claimed by a DYNAMICALLY-MAPPED, per-file pod
— a genuinely orphaned lease (the claiming pod died without ever completing) can only be detected
and reclaimed by a LATER, DIFFERENT pod noticing the expiry. `DBT_BUILD` has no equivalent
multi-pod-race shape: it is one non-mapped task per DagRun, and Airflow's own `retries=2` on that
task IS the reclaim mechanism — a retried attempt re-runs `mark_dbt_build_running`, which
unconditionally `ON CONFLICT`-overwrites the row back to `RUNNING` (plan 09-04's own upsert, no
claimability predicate to fail against). D-17's INTENT — automatic, no operator confirmation gate —
is satisfied by this different-but-equivalent mechanism; this is a deliberate, reasoned divergence
from D-17's literal "lease" wording, not an unaddressed gap, and is recorded here for traceability
rather than left implicit in the Interfaces section alone.
</objective>

09-04's own upsert). Airflow's task-retry mechanism itself IS the "automatic reclaim" for this
stage shape — document this reasoning inline rather than building a redundant lease timer
`DBT_BUILD` structurally does not need (dbt_build is a single non-mapped task per DagRun, not a
dynamically-mapped fan-out where a genuinely orphaned lease from a DIFFERENT pod could occur).

`helm/values/local/monitoring.yaml`'s `freshness-warn`/`freshness-fail` rules (verified this
session, lines ~503-596) are the exact A (SQL, `datasourceUid: analytics-postgres`, `format:

params: [0]}`) three-step rule shape to copy for this plan's new rule, including `for: 5m`
(Pitfall 6: a `FAILED` row does not by itself mean retries are exhausted — gate on persistence, not
raw status).
