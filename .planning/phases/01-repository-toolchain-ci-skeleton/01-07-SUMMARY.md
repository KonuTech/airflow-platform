# Phase 1 Plan 07: Structural Fixture Corpus Summary

Thirty-one structural fixtures — dialect and quoting traps, header/metadata/
footer layouts, and row-shape anomalies — added as declarations only, with the
eleven assertions that prove the bytes have the shape the manifest claims.

## What was built

**Task 1 — dialect and quoting (11 fixtures)** (`e897fae`)

`02_semicolon`, `03_pipe`, `04_tab`, `08_quoted_fields`, `09_embedded_commas`,
`10_embedded_newlines`, `35_quote_in_unquoted_field`,
`36_doubled_vs_backslash_escape`,
`37_delimiter_frequency_differs_header_vs_body`,
`38_single_column_no_delimiter`, `66_triple_nasty`.

The three the plan flagged for care behaved as predicted, and each was verified
against the stdlib parser on CPython 3.12.3 before its expectation was written:

- **38** contains no comma, semicolon, pipe or tab at all, so there is genuinely
  nothing to detect. Its expectation is `detection_declines: true` and
  `detected_delimiter: null`. Separately recorded: `csv.Sniffer` does not
  decline, it raises `Could not determine delimiter`. Those are different
  answers, and the distinction matters because a reader that catches the
  exception and falls back to `,` has converted a refusal into a guess.
- **37** carries three commas and two semicolons in its header and zero commas
  and two semicolons in every body row. A first-line frequency heuristic picks
  `,`; the correct answer is `;`, and the expectation names the *body* as the
  evidence — with `;` every row has three fields, with `,` the header has four
  and every body row has one.
- **66** puts a delimiter, a newline and a doubled-quote escape inside one
  field. Its expectation enumerates the exact parsed record
  `["1", "a,b\nc\"d", "all three at once"]`, so a reader that handles each
  hazard alone fails visibly rather than approximately.

**36** turned out to be the most interesting of the eleven. Row 2 is `"a\"b"`.
Under the doubled-quote convention the `"` after the backslash *closes* the
field and the value is `a\b"`; under a backslash convention the same eight
bytes read as `a"b`. Neither raises. Both readings are declared, because the
finding is that choosing wrong is a silent data corruption rather than a parse
error — which is the whole argument for `csv.escapechar` being a contract
parameter.

**Task 2 — header, metadata and footer layout (9 fixtures)** (`5032b2a`)

`11_no_header`, `12_metadata_before_header`, `13_footer`, `18_empty`,
`19_only_header`, `48_duplicate_header_names_case_variant`,
`49_header_with_leading_trailing_spaces`, `63_repeated_header_mid_file`,
`64_footer_totals_with_different_column_count`.

Every one declares a header row index — `null` for 11 and 18, `4` for 12, `0`
for the rest — plus the contract parameter that overrides detection, because
CSV-07/CSV-08 say the answer is configurable *or* detected and a detector with
no override cannot be corrected at three in the morning.

18 and 19 differ in their *expectations*, not merely in byte count: 18 declares
`schema_is_unknown` and outcome `empty-file`; 19 declares `schema_is_known` and
outcome `header-only-no-records`, plus
`is_a_valid_empty_increment_not_an_error`. Both report "0 rows"; only one of
them is a problem.

One thing the plan did not anticipate and 12 now records: the preamble contains
`# Generated: 2026-01-15`, and a colon is one of CSV-04's five dialects. So the
preamble must be excluded *before* delimiter detection, not after — otherwise
three comment lines vote on the dialect of a file they are not part of.

**Task 3 — row shape, plus the structural assertions (11 fixtures)** (`44a1e0b`)

`14_duplicate_columns`, `15_missing_columns`, `16_extra_columns`,
`17_malformed_rows`, `25_duplicate_rows`, `33_ragged_rows`,
`34_unclosed_quote_eof`, `45_trailing_delimiter_every_row`,
`46_no_trailing_newline`, `47_blank_lines_interspersed`, `65_very_wide`.

The rule the whole group encodes: **no row is ever repaired.** Not one
expectation permits padding a short row, truncating a long one, or coercing a
malformed one into shape. Every anomaly is a rejected record carrying its source
row number and a named reason.

- **33** is ragged in both directions in one file — field counts 4, 3, 5, 2, 6,
  4 around a modal 4, so the distinct set spans 2 through 6 with two values on
  each side. A file that were only short would pass a reader that pads; only
  long would pass one that truncates.
- **34**'s open quote consumes four further lines: six physical lines, three
  parsed records. Both numbers are declared because the *gap* is the finding,
  and the stdlib returns that short result without raising.
- **17** carries a deliberate near-miss: row 2 has a bare quote inside an
  unquoted field, parses correctly, and is **not** a rejection. Without it a
  reader could pass this fixture by rejecting anything unusual, which is the
  other way to lose data.
- **65** is 1 024 columns wide and two rows deep, deliberately below
  PostgreSQL's 1 600-column limit so any failure belongs to the reader.

`tests/unit/test_corpus_structural_fixtures.py` makes eleven assertions, each
comparing the parsed bytes against the fixture's own `expect:` block rather than
restating the expectation in Python — a test with two copies of the truth has no
way to notice when they disagree. Six are the ones the plan named
(field-count distribution, record-vs-line gap, phantom trailing column, missing
final terminator, column width, distinct-row count); five more cover the
empty/header-only pair, the undetectable single column, the triple-nasty values,
and the header-versus-body delimiter disagreement.

## Deviations from Plan

### Auto-fixed issues

**1. [Rule 2 — Missing critical] one of the eleven task-1 declarations did not name its delimiter**

- **Found during:** Task 1, running the acceptance check.
- **Issue:** Task 1's acceptance criteria require every one of the eleven
  declarations to name the delimiter and the record count.
  `36_doubled_vs_backslash_escape` was written with a full escape-convention
  expectation but no `detected_delimiter`, because the fixture is *about*
  quoting rather than dialect. An assertion loop over the eleven caught it.
- **Fix:** added `detected_delimiter: ","`. The criterion is not cosmetic —
  Phase 6's detector loop is parametrised over these blocks, and a fixture with
  no declared delimiter is simply skipped by it.
- **Files:** `tests/fixtures/corpus.yaml`
- **Commit:** `e897fae`

### Additions beyond the plan

**2. [Addition] `65_very_wide.csv` was written by a throwaway script**

A 1 024-column header is 5 036 characters. The plan says the fixture must exceed
a thousand columns and prohibits touching the generator, which leaves exactly
one route: a `literal` fixture whose content is spelled out in the manifest.
(`tabular` is not an option — the model has no default `row_spec`, so it would
need 1 024 explicit column entries.) A human typing that gets the count wrong,
so it was generated by a short script that asserted the comma count on every
line, and the script was deleted after it ran. The manifest entry is the
artifact; `test_the_very_wide_fixture_exceeds_a_thousand_columns` re-derives the
count from the generated bytes, so nothing depends on the script having been
correct.

Most fields are empty, which is both what a wide export actually looks like and
what keeps the declaration at 7 kB rather than 14 kB. The first and last value
of each row are named so an off-by-one at either edge is visible.

**3. [Addition] a YAML quoting convention, written down in the manifest**

The eighteen literal fixtures added here use SINGLE-quoted `content:` scalars,
whereas 32 and 39 use the double-quoted form. Both end at identical bytes; the
difference is which layer resolves the escape. In a single-quoted scalar YAML
does no escape processing at all, so `\n` reaches `_decode_content` as two
characters and a `"` is just a `"`. CSV content is mostly quote characters, and
spelling every one of them as `\"` is how a fixture acquires a quote nobody
meant to write — the same class of invisible-damage trap 01-06 hit with pasted
zero-width characters. The convention and its one remaining sharp edge (a
literal backslash is still `\\`, because the loader's resolution runs either
way) are documented in the manifest header, and every resolved `content` was
printed and inspected before the fixtures were committed.

**4. [Addition] 12's preamble is also a dialect hazard**

`# Generated: 2026-01-15` contains a colon, and CSV-04 names the colon as one of
the five dialects. The expectation now states
`preamble_must_be_excluded_before_dialect_detection: true`, which is a stronger
claim than "the header is at row 4" and is the one a detector sampling from byte
zero gets wrong.

**5. [Addition] deliberate near-misses inside two damaged fixtures**

17 contains a row that parses correctly (a bare quote in an unquoted field), and
49 contains a clean header column alongside two padded ones. Both exist so that
"handles the damage" and "rejects everything that looks unusual" produce
different results. A fixture in which every row is broken cannot tell those two
readers apart.

**6. [Addition] `expect:` blocks name six more Phase-8 quarantine reasons**

`duplicate-header-names`, `field-count-below-header`,
`field-count-above-header`, `unclosed-quote-at-eof` and `empty-file`, plus the
contract parameters `csv.header_row`, `csv.skip_footer_rows`,
`csv.header_case_sensitive`, `csv.header_trim` and `csv.escapechar`. These are
writable today only because 01-03 kept `expect:` permissive; recording them here
means Phase 8 inherits a vocabulary rather than inventing one per fixture.

**7. [Addition] a sensitivity probe on the new test module**

Following the rule established in 01-05 that a control never observed failing is
indistinguishable from a disabled one: both long rows of `33_ragged_rows` were
shortened in the real manifest, and
`test_the_ragged_fixture_is_ragged_in_both_directions` failed naming the empty
set above the modal count. The manifest was reverted and `make fixtures-verify`
confirmed the oracle still matched.
