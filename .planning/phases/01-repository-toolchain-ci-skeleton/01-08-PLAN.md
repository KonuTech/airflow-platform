<objective>
Author the final seventeen fixtures — numeric, temporal, null and boolean damage — and close the
corpus at all sixty-nine names.

Purpose: these are the cases where a value arrives already destroyed by the system that exported
it. What matters is not parsing them but declaring, before any normaliser exists, which ones are
recoverable and which must be rejected. That decision is what stops a future implementer from
"helpfully" coercing a corrupted identifier into a plausible number.
Output: the complete corpus, with the decision-pinning expectations written down.
</objective>

    types the column as a fixed-width string.

    file. The currency-and-percent fixture carries symbol-prefixed and symbol-suffixed values with
    a grouping separator. The sentinel fixture carries the numeric values that conventionally mean
    absent. All three declare the normalised result their expectations require.

    Write every numeric expectation as an exact decimal literal in quotes. A binary floating-point
    expectation in a corpus that exists to test numeric fidelity would be self-defeating.
